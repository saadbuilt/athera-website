§ 01

The cooling crisis is accelerating.

Global temperatures are rising at an unprecedented rate, driving a surge in cooling demand that strains energy grids and accelerates climate change. Mechanical HVAC systems already consume a massive share of the world’s electricity.

The result is a destructive loop. Every air conditioner dumps its heat back into the street; every kilowatt it draws adds carbon to the sky. Dense urban areas now run 3–5 °C hotter than the countryside around them, driven by heat-absorbing surfaces and the waste heat of the machines meant to fix them.

Cooling our buildings heats our cities — and fuels the very crisis we are trying to solve.

The most sustainable cooling strategy is not a bigger air conditioner. It is stopping buildings from absorbing heat in the first place.

§ 02

Three layers. One continuous shield.

ATHERA tackles heat gain across the entire building envelope. Each layer exploits a different property of titanium dioxide (TiO2) to reflect, scatter or radiate heat — intercepting solar radiation at every surface before it reaches the interior.

A

TiO2 ventilated roof tiles

Anatase TiO2-coated ceramic tiles reflect a high proportion of incoming solar radiation. Beneath them, a ventilated air gap carries heat away from the ceiling slab by natural convection — no fans, no electricity.

  • High solar reflectance
  • Passive convective ventilation gap
  • Photocatalytic self-cleaning prevents albedo decay
B

TiO2 exterior wall coating

A photonic coating that backscatters UV and near-infrared rays — blocking over 50% of solar heat gain at the wall surface — while photo-oxidising ambient NOx and VOC pollutants.

  • Rutile TiO2 formulation for maximum reflectance
  • Air-purifying photocatalytic action
  • Durable, low-maintenance finish
C

Flexible radiative window films

TiO2 nanoparticles embedded in a PDMS (polydimethylsiloxane) matrix scatter solar heat while transmitting daylight. The PDMS host radiates internal heat outward as long-wave infrared through the atmospheric window.

  • High visible-light transmittance
  • Passive radiative heat rejection
  • Flexible, retrofit-friendly application

Why it stays white

Reflective roofs usually fail slowly. Dust, soot and biofilm darken them, and every lost point of albedo is heat let back in.

TiO2’s photocatalytic surface breaks down organic contaminants under ordinary sunlight, so the tile cleans itself. Reflectance on day one is close to reflectance in year ten — which is what makes the energy savings bankable.

§ 03

Reflect the sun.
Radiate to space.

Sunlight arrives mostly between 0.3 and 2.5 µm. A warm surface glows back in the far infrared — and between roughly 8 and 13 µm the atmosphere is nearly transparent. Heat emitted in that band escapes straight to the cold of outer space.

0.3–0.4 µm

Ultraviolet

Backscattered by the rutile wall coating — and harvested by anatase surfaces to drive self-cleaning photocatalysis.

0.4–0.7 µm

Visible

Reflected off roofs and walls; transmitted through the window film so interiors keep their daylight and views.

0.7–2.5 µm

Near-infrared

Roughly half of the sun’s energy. Scattered by TiO2 nanoparticles before it can warm the envelope.

8–13 µm

Long-wave IR

Emitted by the PDMS host and leaves through the sky’s transparency window — cooling that runs on physics, not power.

Benefits across three dimensions

By intercepting heat directly at the building surface, ATHERA breaks the link between solar radiation and carbon emissions.

i. Environmental
Lower peak demand eases grid stress. Reduced HVAC load cuts operational carbon. At scale, it directly mitigates the urban heat island.
ii. Economic
Substantial energy savings over the building lifecycle, lower maintenance thanks to self-cleaning surfaces, and modular deployment that allows phased investment.
iii. Social
Better indoor thermal comfort in housing, schools, hospitals and public facilities — most critically in heat-vulnerable communities and regions with limited grid access.
